## Differences found between the courseware and the Directions

- FALLCR: the courseware says 9 per cent of NDTL; the Directions say 16 per cent (MSF 2 per cent). The Directions are taught.
- Scope: the courseware describes standalone application with consolidation as an aim; the Directions apply the LCR and NSFR to Indian banks on standalone and consolidated levels.
- The Directions add changes from 1 April 2026 (Level 1 Government security haircuts, 2.5 percentage points more run-off on retail deposits enabled for internet and mobile banking, deposits pledged against loans), not in the courseware. They are taught as stated in the Directions.
- The courseware gives no run-off rates; 5 and 10 per cent for stable and less stable retail deposits come from the Directions' liquidity return.

## Limits of the evidence

- The copy of the Directions read is a mirror of the 28 November 2025 text; later circulars were not searched for.
- Run-off and inflow rates other than retail deposits and the 75 per cent inflow cap are not taught or tested.
- The courseware's historical phase-in, pandemic relaxation, NSFR dates and its statement on the minimum capital adequacy ratio are the courseware's account; return submission time limits are not tested.
- Intraday liquidity and interest rate risk (same Directions) are not covered here.
- The courseware's own illustrations are not reproduced; all figures are new.
- The reviewer is the authoring agent; no independent or human review.
